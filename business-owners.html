How It Works

Corp FlexMethod:
One Policy, Several Jobs

The business owns a cash-value life insurance policy on a key person (often called corporate-owned life insurance, or COLI). Structured properly and documented by your advisors, it can serve several purposes at once.

01

Protect the Business

If the insured key person dies, the death benefit is paid to the business to cover lost profit, recruiting and debt. The employee must give written notice and consent before the policy is issued for the benefit to generally remain income-tax free.

02

Build Cash Value

Part of each premium builds cash value that grows tax-deferred. In an indexed policy, interest credits are tied to a market index, subject to a cap, with a floor that protects against index losses. Premiums for business-owned coverage are generally not tax-deductible.

03

Help Fund a Key-Employee Benefit

The business can promise selected employees a supplemental retirement benefit that vests over time, and use policy values to help pay it. The promise is deferred compensation and must be documented under tax rule 409A.

04

Help Fund an Owner Buyout

For partners, policies can fund a buy-sell agreement: the death benefit can pay the purchase price at death, and cash value can help with a buyout during life. Who owns the policy affects taxes, so your attorney should decide the structure.

Keeping Your Best People

Give Key Employees
a Reason to Stay.

Replacing a key employee costs far more than a job posting: recruiting, training, lost productivity and lost relationships. A supplemental retirement promise that vests over time gives your best people a concrete reason to build their career with you.

  • A written retirement benefit for selected key employees, separate from your 401(k)
  • Vesting schedule: leave early and the unvested benefit stays with the business
  • The business-owned policy can help fund the promise and can help the business recover its costs
  • Some policies offer chronic or terminal illness riders, depending on the insurance company and state
  • Documented as deferred compensation under tax rule 409A by your attorney
Not guaranteed
Benefit amounts depend on funding, ages, health and actual policy performance. We don't quote figures until we've seen your specific case.
A business obligation
