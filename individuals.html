Personal IUL Planning for High Earners

You've Maxed Your 401(k).
Now What?

If you earn a high income, you may have outgrown the usual retirement accounts. A properly structured indexed universal life (IUL) policy adds life insurance protection, cash value that grows tax-deferred, and a potential source of tax-advantaged money later, when it's designed and managed correctly. It isn't right for everyone, and it takes years of steady funding. We'll show you the trade-offs plainly.

The Process

How the Strategy
Actually Works

This isn't complicated. Four steps — and most of the work is done for you.

01

We Run Your Numbers

A free 30-minute session where we look at your income, tax bracket, and current retirement accounts. No obligation — just an honest look at what the strategy could do for you specifically.

02

We Design the Policy

A properly structured IUL is designed to keep the death benefit at the lowest level the tax code allows for your funding, so more goes to cash value. We compare options from multiple insurance companies for your age, health and goals.

03

You Fund It Strategically

You fund the policy over time: lump sum, annual or monthly. Interest credits are tied to a market index, subject to a cap, with a floor that protects against index losses. Policy charges are deducted every year.

04

You Access Cash Value Later

In retirement, you can take policy loans or withdrawals. When the policy is properly managed and stays in force, loans are generally not taxable income. Loans accrue interest and reduce cash value and death benefit, so we review the policy with you every year.

Important Information

Indexed universal life (IUL) and other cash-value life insurance is life insurance. It is not an investment, a bank account or a security, it is not FDIC insured, and it is not right for everyone.

Costs and risks

  • Policies have fees and charges, highest in the early years. Surrender charges apply for a number of years, so you can get back less than you paid in if you cancel early.
  • Indexed interest credits are subject to caps and participation rates the insurance company can change, and may be zero. The 0% floor applies to index credits only; fees can still reduce cash value.
  • A policy may need more premium than planned to stay in force. Coverage requires medical and financial underwriting.

Taxes and policy loans

  • Cash value grows tax-deferred, and the death benefit is generally income-tax free.
  • Policy loans and withdrawals accrue interest and reduce cash value and death benefit. They may be taxable if the policy lapses, is surrendered or is a modified endowment contract (MEC).
  • We do not give tax or legal advice. Talk with your own tax and legal advisors.

Illustrations and guarantees

  • Illustrations and examples are hypothetical and not guaranteed. Always review the guaranteed values.
  • Guarantees are based on the claims-paying ability of the issuing insurance company.
  • Replacing an existing policy may not be in your best interest; it can mean new surrender charges, costs, underwriting and a new contestability period.
