Your Options

There's More Than One
Way Out of Debt

Every option has trade-offs. A good review walks you through the ones that fit your situation, without pushing you toward any one of them.

☎️

Ask your card company

Many card companies offer hardship plans with lower rates or payments. It's free to call the number on the back of your card. Trade-off: not every lender says yes, and the card may be frozen.

🗓️

Debt management plan

A credit counseling agency works with your creditors to lower interest rates, and you make one monthly payment. Trade-off: usually 3–5 years, with small monthly fees, and cards are typically closed.

🧾

Consolidation loan

One fixed-rate loan pays off several cards. Trade-off: it only helps if the new rate is lower and you don't run the cards back up. It usually needs decent credit.

🤝

Debt settlement

Negotiating with creditors to accept less than the full balance. Trade-off: it can seriously hurt your credit, isn't guaranteed, and forgiven debt may be taxable.

⚖️

Bankruptcy

A legal process that can discharge or restructure debt. Trade-off: serious, long-lasting credit impact. It's usually a last resort and needs a bankruptcy attorney.

📊

A budget and a payoff plan

Sometimes the answer is a clear plan: which debt to pay first and what to cut. Trade-off: it takes discipline, but it costs nothing and protects your credit.

The Honest Truth

Debt relief can be a real fresh start. It can also be a trap. Here's how to tell the difference.

  • No legitimate company charges you before it does anything. Under federal rules, debt relief companies that sell by phone can't collect fees until they've actually settled or changed a debt. An upfront fee is a red flag.
  • Settlement can hurt your credit. Many settlement programs involve stopping payments while money is saved, which leads to late payments, fees and possible collections or lawsuits.
  • Forgiven debt may be taxable. If a creditor forgives $600 or more, you may receive a 1099-C, and the IRS may count it as income. Ask a tax professional.
  • No one can guarantee results. Creditors don't have to agree to anything. Be wary of anyone promising a specific percentage off your debt.

Our promise: We'll never pressure you, never charge you for the review, and never make help with debt depend on buying insurance. If a debt relief offer doesn't feel right, report it at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.
